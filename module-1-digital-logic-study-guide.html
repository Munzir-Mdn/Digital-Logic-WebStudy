SECR1013 Digital Logic — Module 1: Introductory Concepts

1. Chapter Overview

This module introduces analog and digital quantities, binary logic levels, digital waveforms, frequency and period, pulse width and duty cycle, timing diagrams, data transfer, basic logic gates and functions, fixed-function integrated circuits, and programmable logic devices (PLDs).

Foundation: Analog quantities vary continuously. Digital systems use discrete values and represent logic using HIGH/LOW states, corresponding to binary 1/0.

2. Key Concepts

  • Analog: continuously varying quantities.
  • Digital: discrete values; digital systems can efficiently process, store and transmit data.
  • ADC: converts analog signals to digital using sampling and quantization; information loss can occur.
  • DAC: converts digital data to analog signals, e.g. for an analog speaker.
  • Logic levels: HIGH and LOW represent binary states.
  • Periodic waveform: repeats after a fixed period; aperiodic does not.
  • Frequency: cycles per second (Hz). Period: time for one cycle.
  • Duty cycle: fraction of a period spent in the active/on state.
  • Timing diagram: shows relationships among two or more digital waveforms.
  • Data transfer: serial or parallel.
  • Logic operations: AND, OR and NOT are foundational; the module also summarizes NAND, NOR, XOR and XNOR.
  • Logic functions: comparison, arithmetic, code conversion, encoding/decoding, data selection, storage and counting.
  • IC packages: DIP, SOIC, PLCC and LCCC; IC technologies include TTL, ECL, CMOS, NMOS and BiCMOS.
  • PLD: user-programmable logic; major types shown are SPLD, CPLD and FPGA.

4. What to Remember & How It Works

Analog → ADC → digital processing/storage → DAC → analog output is the central hybrid-system flow illustrated using audio/CD examples. Digital waveforms transition between LOW and HIGH. A pulse has amplitude, pulse width and transition times; repetitive pulse trains additionally have period, frequency and duty cycle.

A comparator determines relationships between binary numbers. Arithmetic functions are based on adders. Encoders/decoders convert representations, MUX/DEMUX select and route data, flip-flops/registers/memory store data, and counters produce binary sequences representing counted input pulses.

Fixed-function ICs perform predetermined functions. PLDs differ because their logic function is programmed by the user; the module introduces graphical/schematic entry and HDL-based entry such as ABEL, CUPL and Verilog.

5. Common Mistakes

  • Confusing frequency with period; always use reciprocal units correctly.
  • Forgetting to convert ms, µs, ns or ps before calculations.
  • Using total period instead of pulse width in the duty-cycle numerator.
  • Reading timing diagrams at transition boundaries rather than within the specified bit interval.
  • Confusing serial transfer (bits sequentially) with parallel transfer (multiple bits simultaneously).
  • Confusing a fixed-function IC with a user-programmable PLD.

6. Study Priorities

  1. Master analog vs digital and ADC/DAC.
  2. Memorize HIGH/LOW and basic gate behavior.
  3. Practice f ↔ T unit conversions until automatic.
  4. Practice duty-cycle calculations and timing-diagram reading.
  5. Learn the purpose of comparator, adder, encoder/decoder, MUX/DEMUX, storage and counter blocks.
  6. Differentiate IC packages/technologies and SPLD/CPLD/FPGA.

7. Real-World Applications

The slides connect digital systems with computers, manufacturing, medical science, transportation, entertainment and telecommunications. Counting examples include traffic lights, washing machines, vending machines and ATMs. Hybrid audio systems demonstrate ADC/DAC conversion.

8. Practice Questions + Model Answers

Q1. Which quantity varies continuously: analog or digital?

Answer: Analog.

Q2. A signal has T = 10 ms. Find f.

Answer: 10 ms = 0.01 s; f = 1/0.01 = 100 Hz.

Q3. A system has T = 1000 ms and duty cycle = 25%. Find ON and OFF time.

Answer: ON = 0.25×1000 = 250 ms; OFF = 1000−250 = 750 ms.

Q4. A system lasts 500 ms with a 40% duty cycle. Find pulse width and OFF time.

Answer: tW = 0.40×500 = 200 ms; OFF = 300 ms.

Q5. State the conditions for AND, OR and NOT.

Answer: AND is true only when all input conditions are true; OR is true when one or more inputs are true; NOT produces the opposite condition.

Q6. Explain ADC and DAC in a hybrid audio system.

Answer: ADC converts the analog audio signal into digital form for digital processing/storage. DAC converts digital data back into an analog signal so an analog speaker can reproduce sound.

Q7. Name the three major PLD groups presented.

Answer: SPLD, CPLD and FPGA.

Short essay. Why are mixed analog/digital systems useful?

Model answer: They combine the strengths of both technologies. Real-world signals are often analog, while digital systems efficiently process, store and transmit data. ADC enables conversion into digital form and DAC enables conversion back to analog output when required.

9. Final Recap

Focus on the chain analog/digital → logic levels → waveforms → frequency/period → pulse/duty cycle → timing/data transfer → gates/functions → ICs → PLDs. These ideas form the foundation for later Digital Logic topics.
