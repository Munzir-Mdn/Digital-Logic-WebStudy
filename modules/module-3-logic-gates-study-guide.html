10. Timing Diagram Strategy

  1. Split the waveform at every input transition.
  2. For each interval, write A, B (and C if present) as 0 or 1.
  3. Apply the gate rule independently to each interval.
  4. Draw X HIGH or LOW for the whole interval.
Fast check: AND looks for all 1s; NAND is its inverse. OR looks for at least one 1; NOR is its inverse. XOR checks different; XNOR checks same.

12. Practice + Answers

Q1. Input A to NOT is LOW. Output?

Answer: HIGH (1).

Q2. All inputs of an AND gate are LOW. Output?

Answer: LOW (0).

Q3. How many input combinations exist for a 4-input gate?

Answer: 2⁴ = 16.

Q4. A=1, B=0. Find AND, OR, NAND, NOR, XOR, XNOR.

Answer: AND=0, OR=1, NAND=1, NOR=0, XOR=1, XNOR=0.

Q5. For a 3-input NAND, A=B=C=1. Output?

Answer: AND would be 1, then invert → 0.

Q6. For a 3-input NOR, A=B=C=0. Output?

Answer: OR would be 0, then invert → 1.

Q7. What distinguishes XOR and XNOR?

Answer: XOR outputs 1 when the two inputs are different; XNOR outputs 1 when they are the same.

13. Common Mistakes

  • Reading Boolean “+” as normal arithmetic addition rather than OR.
  • Forgetting the inversion bubble on NAND/NOR.
  • Thinking OR requires both inputs HIGH; it requires any HIGH input.
  • Confusing XOR with OR when A=B=1: OR=1 but XOR=0.
  • Confusing XNOR with XOR: XNOR detects equal inputs.
  • Trying to solve a timing diagram visually without dividing it into intervals.
